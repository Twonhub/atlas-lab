Atlas Lab — Quick Start

This interactive anatomy website runs in a WebGL-enabled laptop browser; visitors do not need Python.

Move around the 3D anatomy — no tools to switch

  1. Left-click and drag the model to orbit left, right, up or down. Just click a structure to select it; click again to deselect.
  2. Right-click and drag (or Shift + left-drag) to pan your view in any direction without rotating.
  3. Mouse wheel or trackpad pinch/scroll to zoom in on a particular part of the body. On a touch screen, use one finger to rotate or two fingers to pan/zoom.
  4. Option/Alt + left-drag directly on a selected mesh to pull it away, without switching navigation modes.
  5. Keyboard shortcuts: arrow keys orbit around the current focus; W/A/S/D pan up/left/down/right; +/- zoom; F toggles fullscreen.
  6. Keep Whole body selected, then use Dissect / Separate → Whole body — separate all parts to spread the anatomy. Move the slider to 0% or click Reassemble to restore the original positions.
  7. Select a Body Region such as Wrist, Skull or Heart to jump there automatically. Choose Left/Right/Both as appropriate, then switch the anatomical system to examine its structures. If a requested system has no meshes there, the app explains the limitation and shows skeleton instead.
  8. Choose Anatomical View for anterior, posterior, left/right lateral, superior, inferior or oblique positions. The camera maintains its current focus and distance.

Learn structure and function

Selected structures display a short explanation and function in the model inspector. Choose Anatomy Library for reference-linked pages describing each curated structure, its purpose, its nearby relationships and study landmarks. You can search by structure name, system, region, or function, then choose Locate in 3D.

What the explanations cover

The library has dozens of authored anatomy entries linked to the free OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology textbook. The source atlas has many more individual mesh labels than the library. For structures without an individual entry, the inspector provides clearly designated general category information; it does not invent specific muscle actions, nerve supply or pathology.

Model credits and accuracy

The source geometry derives from the licensed open atlas. The medical descriptions are educational summaries, not an independent clinical audit of every model label. Read accuracy review and model attribution. Atlas Lab must not be used for diagnosis or medical procedures.
